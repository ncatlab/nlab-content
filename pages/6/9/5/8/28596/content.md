## Idea

In [[quantum computing]] and [[quantum information theory]], *magic states* are a kind of [[quantum states]] that are (envisioned to be) used as a quantum resource in order to retain viability of [[quantum error correction]] by [[surface codes]] or similar means.

The *[[Clifford group]]* of [[quantum gates]] on $n$ [[qbits]] (the [[normalizer subgroup|normalizer]] of the [[Pauli group]] in $U(2^n)$, generated by the [[Hadamard gate]] $H$, the phase gate $S = diag(1,\mathrm{i})$ and the [[CNOT gate]]) is computationally weak: By the *[Gottesman-Knill theorem](Clifford+group#GottesmanKnillTheorem), Clifford [[quantum circuits]] acting on stabilizer states and followed by Pauli [[quantum measurements]] are efficiently simulable on a classical computer. But adjoining any one non-Clifford gate yields a [universal gate set](quantum+logic+gate#UniversalGateSets). 

The prominent choice of that extra non-Clifford gate is the *[[T-gate]]* $T = \mathrm{diag}(1, e^{\pi \mathrm{i}/4})$: The set Clifford+$T$ generates, up to global phase, a [[dense subset|dense]] [[subgroup]] of [[special unitary group|$SU(2^n)$]], so that every [[quantum circuit]] is efficiently approximated by Clifford+$T$ circuits (by the [[Solovay-Kitaev theorem]]).
 
The catch is that: 

* *The prominent topological [[quantum error correction|quantum error-correcting codes]] like the [[surface code]] (lattice [[quantum simulations]] of [[topological order]]) protect only [[Clifford gates]].* And this is a problem of principle ([Eastin & Knill 2009](#EastinKnill09), [Bravyi & König 2013](#BravyiKoenig13)).

* Also actual [[topological quantum computation]] as long as its [[anyons]] are only of [[Ising anyons|Ising type]] (such as expected, but not established, for the [$\nu = 5/2$](quantum+Hall+effect#ReferencesOnNuFiveHalves) [[FQH]] states) yields exactly only the Clifford gates ([Bravyi 2006](#Bravyi06), [Nayak et al. 2008, §IV.A](#NayakSimonSternFreedmanDasSarma08)). 


The now standard strategy to work around this problem, trades the missing gate for a resource state: $T$-gates are enacted by *gate teleportation*, consuming *[[magic states]]* $\vert A \rangle = T \vert + \rangle$ via a [[CNOT gate|CNOT]], a [[Pauli gate|$Z$-]][[quantum measurement|measurement]] and a [[controlled quantum gate|classically controlled]] $S$-correction (which are all Clifford operations). 

These *[[magic states]]*, in turn, are encoded without protection ("state injection") and then purified by *magic state distillation* ([Bravyi & Kitaev 2005](#BravyiKitaev05)), a protocol which uses only Clifford operations and measurements to turn many noisy copies into fewer, better ones. 
 
The resulting architecture --- the surface code as a protected "Clifford substrate", fed with non-Clifford resources by dedicated "magic state factories" --- is expensive: A $T$-gate costs a few hundred times a Clifford gate in space-time overhead ([Campbell, Terhal & Vuillot 2017, §II.5](#CampbellTerhalVuillot17)), and in the estimate of [Fowler et al. 2012](#FowlerMariantoniMartinisCleland12) for factoring, magic state production occupies over 94% of the physical qubits. 

Accordingly, the "$T$-count" (number of [[T-gates]] in a [[quantum circuit]]) has become a standard cost measure of quantum algorithms, and much current work aims to reduce this overhead by various proposed methods:

1. by more efficient distillation ([Litinski 2019](#Litinski19)); 

1. by *magic state cultivation*, which grows a $T$-state inside a single surface-code patch at roughly the cost of a lattice-surgery CNOT and may make distillation unnecessary in practice ([Gidney, Shutty & Jones 2024](#GidneyShuttyJones24)); 

1. by circumventing the 2-dimensional no-go theorem, either via 3-dimensional codes with transversal $T$ ([Bombín 2015](#Bombin15)) or via non-constant-depth protocols on the surface code itself ([Brown 2020](#Brown20)). For review see [Terhal 2015, §II.7](#Terhal15), [Campbell, Terhal & Vuillot 2017](#CampbellTerhalVuillot17).
