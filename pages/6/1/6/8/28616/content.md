## Research

My research lies at the intersection of [[mathematical logic]], [[dependent type theory]], and [[higher category theory|higher-dimensional mathematics]], with a particular interest in the relationship between mathematical meaning and [[formal proof]].

I am interested in how mathematical structures and arguments can be translated between different mathematical languages while preserving their underlying meaning. In particular, I study the semantic faithfulness of AI-assisted mathematical formalization: how a machine-checkable proof can be explicitly related to the mathematical statement and reasoning from which it originates.

My current work involves [[homotopy type theory]], [[higher category theory]], and formalization in Cubical Agda and [[Lean]]. I am also working under the research guidance of [[Urs Schreiber]] on the formalization of [[homotopy theory|homotopy-theoretic]] constructions in [[Cubical Agda]].

More broadly, I am interested in the role of [[higher structure|higher-dimensional structures]] in the foundations of mathematical reasoning, and in how formal methods may reshape the relationship between mathematical proof and mathematical understanding.

## Projects

### Homotopy Theory in Cubical Agda

Under the guidance of [[Urs Schreiber]], I am working on the formalization of homotopy-theoretic constructions in Cubical Agda, with the aim of developing mathematical materials that connect readable mathematical exposition with machine-checked formal proofs.

An initial example concerns [[monodromy]], formulated through transport in dependent type families. The work includes source-grounded formalization, explicit mathematical dependencies, and correspondences between mathematical statements and Agda declarations.

The longer-term goal is to develop an extensible collection of formalized homotopy-theoretic results that can be reviewed, reproduced, and further developed by other mathematicians.

### Semantic Faithfulness and Shadow Mathematics

I am exploring a framework for connecting mathematical exposition with machine-checked formal proofs, particularly in the context of AI-assisted mathematical formalization.

The central idea is to associate formalized statements and proofs with explicit source references, mathematical dependencies, and readable mathematical reconstructions. This provides a basis for examining not only whether a formal proof is accepted by a proof assistant, but also whether it faithfully represents the intended mathematical meaning.

I am particularly interested in the role of human mathematical judgment in this process, and in developing tools that make the interpretation and review of AI-generated formalizations more efficient.
