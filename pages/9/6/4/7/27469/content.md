## Idea

In [[quantum information theory]] and [[quantum computing]], by the *T-gate* and the *S-gate* one refers to the [[quantum gates]] acting on single [[qbits]] that in the defining measurement-[[linear basis|basis]] $\big\{ {\vert 0 \rangle}, {\vert 1 \rangle} \big\}$ are given by the $2 \times 2$ [[complex numbers|complex]] [[matrices]]

$$
  T 
  \;=\;
  \left[
    \begin{array}{cc}
      1 & 0
      \\
      0 & e^{\pi \mathrm{i}/4}
    \end{array}
  \right]
$$

and

$$
  S 
  \;=\; 
  T^2
  \;=\;
  \left[
    \begin{array}{cc}
      1 & 0
      \\
      0 & \mathrm{i}
    \end{array}
  \right]
  \mathrlap{\,,}
$$

respectively, where "$\mathrm{i}$" denotes the [[imaginary unit]].

In particular, the [[Pauli Z-gate]] is decomposable into these gates as 

$$
  Z   
   \;=\; 
  S^2 
   \;=\; 
  T^4
  \,.
$$

\begin{remark}
Beware of these alternative names and their subtleties:

* The T-gate is also known as the "$\pi/8$-gate" (e.g. in [Nielsen & Chuang 2000 p xxx](#NielsenChuang00)), even though the phase rotation is by $\pi/4$ -- but differs by only a global phase from the $R_Z(\pi/8)$ [[rotation gate]].

* The S-gate is also known as the "phase gate", but that term is ambiguous.

\end{remark}


## Properties

### Role in fault-tolerant quantum computing

The *[[Clifford group]]* of [[quantum gates]] on $n$ [[qbits]] (the [[normalizer subgroup|normalizer]] of the [[Pauli group]] in $U(2^n)$, generated by the [[Hadamard gate]] $H$, the phase gate $S = diag(1,\mathrm{i})$ and the [[CNOT gate]]) is computationally weak: By the *[Gottesman-Knill theorem](Clifford+group#GottesmanKnillTheorem), Clifford [[quantum circuits]] acting on stabilizer states and followed by Pauli [[quantum measurements]] are efficiently simulable on a classical computer. But adjoining any one non-Clifford gate yields a [universal gate set](quantum+logic+gate#UniversalGateSets). 

The prominent choice of that extra non-Clifford gate is the *[[T-gate]]* $T = \mathrm{diag}(1, e^{\pi \mathrm{i}/4})$ (but there are other reasonable choices, like the [[CCZ gate]]): The set Clifford+$T$ generates, up to global phase, a [[dense subset|dense]] [[subgroup]] of [[special unitary group|$SU(2^n)$]], so that every [[quantum circuit]] is efficiently approximated by Clifford+$T$ circuits (by the [[Solovay-Kitaev theorem]]).
 
The catch is that: 

* *The prominent topological [[quantum error correction|quantum error-correcting codes]] like the [[surface code]] (lattice [[quantum simulations]] of [[topological order]]) protect only [[Clifford gates]].* And this is a problem of principle ([Eastin & Knill 2009](magic+state#EastinKnill09), [Bravyi & König 2013](magic+state##BravyiKoenig13)).

* Also actual [[topological quantum computation]] as long as its [[anyons]] are only of [[Ising anyons|Ising type]] (such as expected, but not established, for the [$\nu = 5/2$](quantum+Hall+effect#ReferencesOnNuFiveHalves) [[FQH]] states) yields exactly only the Clifford gates ([Bravyi 2006](magic+state##Bravyi06), [Nayak et al. 2008, §IV.A](magic+state##NayakSimonSternFreedmanDasSarma08)). 


The now standard strategy to work around this problem, trades the missing gate for a resource state: $T$-gates are enacted by *gate teleportation*, consuming *[[magic states]]* $\vert A \rangle = T \vert + \rangle$ via a [[CNOT gate|CNOT]], a [[Pauli gate|$Z$-]][[quantum measurement|measurement]] and a [[controlled quantum gate|classically controlled]] $S$-correction (which are all Clifford operations). 

For more see at *[[magic state]]*.
