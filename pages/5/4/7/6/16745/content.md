## Idea
 {#Idea}

The concept of *Borcherds algebras* is a generalization of that of [[Kac-Moody algebras]] (hence also called *generalized Kac-Moody algebras* or *Borcherds-Kac-Moody algebras*, "BKM algebras") obtained by allowing imaginary [[root (in representation theory)|simple roots]].

Recall that a (symmetrizable) [[Kac-Moody algebra]] is presented by [[generators and relations]] as being glued from copies of [[sl(2)|$\mathfrak{sl}(2)$]], one [[triple]] $(e_i, h_i, f_i)$ for each [[simple root]] $\alpha_i$, according to a [[Cartan matrix]] $(a_{i j})$ whose diagonal entries are all $a_{i i} = 2$ and whose off-diagonal entries are non-[[positive number|positive]] [[integers]], subject to the [[Serre relations]]. That all diagonal entries are positive means that all [[simple roots]] have [[positive number|positive]] [[norm]] ("real roots"). This makes the [[adjoint action]] of each of these [[sl(2)|$\mathfrak{sl}(2)$]]-[[triples]] locally finite ("integrable") and yields a [[Weyl group]] generated by the [[reflections]] in all the [[simple roots]].

The idea of [Borcherds 1988](#Borcherds88) is to keep this kind of presentation but to admit also diagonal entries $a_{i i} \leq 0$, hence simple roots $\alpha_i$ of non-positive norm ("imaginary simple roots"). For these:

* the triple $(e_i, h_i, f_i)$ still [[linear span|spans]] a copy of [[sl(2)|$\mathfrak{sl}(2)$]] if $a_{i i} \lt 0$, but a copy of the 3-dimensional [[Heisenberg Lie algebra]] if $a_{i i} = 0$;

* there are no [[Serre relations]] of the form $(\mathrm{ad}_{e_i})^n(e_j) = 0$ (so that $e_i$ need not act locally nilpotently), only the relation $[e_i, e_j] = 0 = [f_i, f_j]$ whenever $a_{i j} = 0$;

* there is no corresponding [[reflection]]: the [[Weyl group]] is generated by the reflections in the *real* simple roots only.

The effect is that Borcherds algebras behave like [[Kac-Moody algebras]] with some "freely generated directions" adjoined: 

For example, if no two imaginary simple roots are orthogonal, then a Borcherds algebra decomposes as a [[direct sum]] $\mathfrak{u}^- \oplus (\mathfrak{g}_J + \mathfrak{h}) \oplus \mathfrak{u}^+$, where $\mathfrak{g}_J$ is the Kac-Moody algebra of the real simple roots, $\mathfrak{h}$ is the [[Cartan subalgebra]], and $\mathfrak{u}^\pm$ are [[free Lie algebras]] ([Jurisich 1998](#Jurisich98)). 

Nevertheless, most of the structure theory of [[Kac-Moody algebras]] carries over ([Borcherds 1988](#Borcherds88), [Kac 1990, §11.13](#Kac90)), notably the [[invariant polynomial|invariant]] [[bilinear form]], the root space decomposition with finite-dimensional root spaces, the Weyl group, and a version of the [[Weyl-Kac character formula]] and its denominator identity, now with an extra correction term contributed by the imaginary simple roots.

More conceptually, Borcherds algebras are essentially characterized as the [[Lie algebras]] carrying an *almost positive definite contravariant form* ([Borcherds 1988, §3](#Borcherds88), [Borcherds 1995a](#Borcherds95a)): If $\mathfrak{g} = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathfrak{g}_n$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-[[graded Lie algebra]] with $\mathfrak{g}_n$ finite-dimensional for $n \neq 0$, equipped with an [[involution]] $\omega$ which maps $\mathfrak{g}_n$ to $\mathfrak{g}_{-n}$ and acts as $-1$ on $\mathfrak{g}_0$, and with an $\omega$-invariant symmetric invariant [[bilinear form]] $(-,-)$ which pairs $\mathfrak{g}_n$ only with $\mathfrak{g}_{-n}$, such that the *contravariant form*
$$
  (x,y)_0 \;\coloneqq\; -\big(x, \omega(y)\big)
$$
is [[positive definite]] on all $\mathfrak{g}_{n \neq 0}$, then the [[kernel]] of $(-,-)$ is [[center|central]] and the [[quotient]] by it is a Borcherds algebra ([Borcherds 1988, Thm. 3.1](#Borcherds88)). 

Conversely, Borcherds algebras satisfy these conditions, up to minor technical caveats ([Borcherds 1995a, §1](#Borcherds95a)). This is just the structure carried by spaces of physical states in [[string theory]], where positivity of the contravariant form is the content of the [[no-ghost theorem]] ([Borcherds 1988, §5](#Borcherds88)). This is how Borcherds algebras were found ([Borcherds 1986](#Borcherds86)) and why they keep appearing in [[string theory]].


## Definition
 {#Definition}

We follow [Borcherds 1995a, §1](#Borcherds95a). All [[Lie algebras]] are [[ground field|over]] the [[real numbers]].

+-- {: .num_defn #BorcherdsCartanMatrix}
###### Definition
**(Borcherds-Cartan matrix)**

A *Borcherds-Cartan matrix* is a real square [[matrix]] $A = (a_{i j})_{i,j \in I}$, indexed by a [[countable set]] $I$, such that:

1. $a_{i j} = a_{j i}$ for all $i, j \in I$;

1. $a_{i j} \leq 0$ for all $i \neq j$;

1. if $a_{i i} \gt 0$ then $2 a_{i j}/a_{i i} \in \mathbb{Z}$ for all $j \in I$.

An index $i \in I$ is called *real* if $a_{i i} \gt 0$, and *imaginary* if $a_{i i} \leq 0$ (*isotropic* if moreover $a_{i i} = 0$).

=--

+-- {: .num_defn #UniversalBorcherdsAlgebra}
###### Definition
**(universal Borcherds algebra)**

Given a Borcherds-Cartan matrix $A$ (Def. \ref{BorcherdsCartanMatrix}), the *universal Borcherds algebra* $\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}(A)$ is the [[Lie algebra]] generated by elements
$$
  e_i,\, f_i,\, h_{i j}
  \,,
  \;\;\;\;
  i, j \in I
  \,,
$$
subject to the following relations, for all $i, j, k \in I$:

1. $[e_i, f_j] = h_{i j}$;

1. $[h_{i j}, e_k] = \delta_{i j}\, a_{i k}\, e_k$ and $[h_{i j}, f_k] = - \delta_{i j}\, a_{i k}\, f_k$;

1. if $a_{i i} \gt 0$ and $j \neq i$, then $(\mathrm{ad}_{e_i})^{1 - 2 a_{i j}/a_{i i}}(e_j) = 0$ and $(\mathrm{ad}_{f_i})^{1 - 2 a_{i j}/a_{i i}}(f_j) = 0$;

1. if $a_{i j} = 0$, then $[e_i, e_j] = 0$ and $[f_i, f_j] = 0$.

=--

+-- {: .num_remark #TheCentralElements}
###### Remark
**(the elements $h_{i j}$)**

The relations of Def. \ref{UniversalBorcherdsAlgebra} imply that the $h_{i j}$ all commute with each other, that the $h_{i j}$ with $i \neq j$ are [[center|central]], and that $h_{i j} = 0$ unless the $i$th and the $j$th column of $A$ coincide ([Borcherds 1991](#Borcherds91), see [Gannon](#Gannon)). Hence, if no two columns of $A$ coincide, then $\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}(A)$ is generated by the $e_i$, $f_i$ and $h_i \coloneqq h_{i i}$ subject to
$$
  [h_i, h_j] = 0
  \,,
  \;\;\;
  [e_i, f_j] = \delta_{i j}\, h_i
  \,,
  \;\;\;
  [h_i, e_j] = a_{i j}\, e_j
  \,,
  \;\;\;
  [h_i, f_j] = - a_{i j}\, f_j
$$
together with items 3 and 4 of Def. \ref{UniversalBorcherdsAlgebra}. This is of the familiar form of the Chevalley-Serre presentation of [[Kac-Moody algebras]]. In general, the quotient of $\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}(A)$ by the $h_{i j}$ with $i \neq j$ is what is often taken as the definition of *the* Borcherds algebra $\mathfrak{g}(A)$ (e.g. [Gannon](#Gannon)). The $h_{i j}$ with $i \neq j$ matter only for [[central extensions]] ([Borcherds 1991](#Borcherds91), [Borcherds 1995a, §1](#Borcherds95a)).

=--

+-- {: .num_defn #BorcherdsAlgebra}
###### Definition
**(Borcherds algebra)**

A *Borcherds algebra* (or *generalized Kac-Moody algebra*) is a Lie algebra of the form
$$
  \mathfrak{g}
  \;=\;
  \mathfrak{a} \rtimes \mathfrak{d}
$$
where

* $\mathfrak{a}$ is the quotient of a universal Borcherds algebra $\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}(A)$ (Def. \ref{UniversalBorcherdsAlgebra}) by a subspace of its [[center]],

* $\mathfrak{d}$ is an [[abelian Lie algebra]] acting on $\mathfrak{a}$ by [[derivations]] such that all the $e_i$ and $f_i$ are eigenvectors.

In other words, Borcherds algebras are obtained from universal Borcherds algebras by discarding part of the center and adjoining commuting outer derivations.

=--

+-- {: .num_remark #RootsAndWeylGroup}
###### Remark
**(roots, Weyl group and contravariant form)**

Let $Q \coloneqq \bigoplus_{i \in I} \mathbb{Z}\, \alpha_i$ be the free abelian group on symbols $\alpha_i$ (the *simple roots*), equipped with the bilinear form given by $(\alpha_i, \alpha_j) \coloneqq a_{i j}$. Declaring $e_i$ to be of degree $\alpha_i$, $f_i$ of degree $-\alpha_i$ and $h_{i j}$ of degree $0$ makes (universal) Borcherds algebras $Q$-[[graded Lie algebra|graded]]. A *root* is a nonzero $\alpha \in Q$ whose degree-$\alpha$ subspace is non-trivial. Every root is a non-negative or a non-positive integer combination of simple roots, and a root is called *real* if $(\alpha, \alpha) \gt 0$ and *imaginary* otherwise ([Borcherds 1988, §1](#Borcherds88)). The root spaces are finite-dimensional, and the simple root spaces are 1-dimensional. Moreover:

* for each $i \in I$ the elements $e_i$, $h_i$, $f_i$ span a copy of $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ if $a_{i i} \neq 0$, and a copy of the 3-dimensional [[Heisenberg Lie algebra]] if $a_{i i} = 0$;

* the *Weyl group* is the group of [[automorphisms]] of $Q$ generated by the reflections $\alpha \mapsto \alpha - \frac{2 (\alpha, \alpha_i)}{a_{i i}} \alpha_i$ in the *real* simple roots $\alpha_i$ only;

* the *Cartan involution* $\omega$ is given by $\omega(e_i) = - f_i$ and $\omega(f_i) = - e_i$, hence $\omega(h_i) = - h_i$.

For the Borcherds algebras as in [Borcherds 1988, §1](#Borcherds88) (with Cartan subalgebra a real vector space $H$ containing the $h_i$, equipped with a symmetric bilinear form with $(h_i, h_j) = a_{i j}$), there is a unique invariant symmetric bilinear form extending that on $H$. The corresponding contravariant form $(x,y)_0 \coloneqq -(x, \omega(y))$ is positive definite on all root spaces ([Borcherds 1988, Cor. 2.5](#Borcherds88), whose proof is completed in [Kac 1990, §11.13](#Kac90), see [Borcherds 1995a, §3](#Borcherds95a)). This is the "almost positivity" from the [Idea](#Idea)-section.

=--

+-- {: .num_remark #VariantsOfTheDefinition}
###### Remark
**(variants of the definition)**

* ([[Kac-Moody algebras]] as special cases) If $a_{i i} \gt 0$ for all $i \in I$, then item 4 in Def. \ref{UniversalBorcherdsAlgebra} follows from item 3 ([Borcherds 1988, §1](#Borcherds88)). After rescaling to $a_{i i} = 2$ (as below), the Borcherds algebras of Def. \ref{BorcherdsAlgebra} are then the central quotients, extended by derivations, of symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebras.

* (normalization) Rescaling the $i$th row of a Borcherds-Cartan matrix by $2/a_{i i}$ for all real $i$ turns it into a symmetrizable matrix with $a_{i i} = 2$ for real $i$, and with $a_{i j} \in \mathbb{Z}$ whenever $a_{i i} = 2$. This is the normalization convention used, e.g., in [Gannon](#Gannon). [Kac 1990, §11.13](#Kac90) moreover admits non-symmetrizable matrices, for which however there is no invariant bilinear form ([Borcherds 1995a, §1](#Borcherds95a)). For symmetrizable matrices, Kac's definition agrees essentially with Def. \ref{BorcherdsAlgebra} (by [Kac 1990, Thm. 11.13.1](#Kac90), see [Borcherds 1995a, §3](#Borcherds95a)).

* (superalgebras) There is an evident generalization to [[Lie superalgebras]], where some of the generators $e_i$, $f_i$ are odd. These *Borcherds superalgebras* (or *generalized Kac-Moody superalgebras*) were envisaged already in [Borcherds 1988, §5](#Borcherds88) and developed in [Ray 1995](#Ray95), [Ray 2006](#Ray06). It is mostly Borcherds superalgebras which appear in [[supergravity]] (see the references [below](#InSupergravity)).

=--




## Examples

### Lie algebras of physical string states
 {#LieAlgebrasOfPhysicalStringStates}

The archetypical examples of Borcherds algebras, which also motivated the concept, are Lie algebras of physical [[quantum states]] of the [[bosonic string]] ([Frenkel 1985](#Frenkel85), [Borcherds 1986](#Borcherds86), [1988, §5](#Borcherds88)). They are constructed from [[vertex operator algebras|vertex algebras]] as follows (for exposition see [Gebert 1993, §3.4](#Gebert93)):

+-- {: .num_defn #LieAlgebraOfPhysicalStates}
###### Definition
**(Lie algebra of physical states)**

Let $V$ be a [[vertex algebra]] with conformal vector, whose [[Virasoro algebra|Virasoro]] modes we denote by $L_n$, and with an invariant symmetric [[bilinear form]]. Its spaces of *physical states* of weight $k$ are
$$
  P^k
  \;\coloneqq\;
  \big\{
    v \in V
    \,\big\vert\,
    L_0 v = k\, v
    ,\;
    L_n v = 0 \;\text{for all}\; n \gt 0
  \big\}
  \,.
$$
Then $L_{-1} P^0 \subset P^1$, and the quotient $P^1 / L_{-1} P^0$ is a [[Lie algebra]] with bracket given by the zero-modes of the vertex operators, $[u, v] \coloneqq u_0 v$ ([Borcherds 1986](#Borcherds86), see [Borcherds 1992, §6](#Borcherds92)). The bilinear form descends to an invariant form on this Lie algebra, and we write
$$
  \mathfrak{g}(V)
  \;\coloneqq\;
  \big( P^1 / L_{-1} P^0 \big) \big/ \mathrm{ker}(-,-)
$$
for its quotient by the kernel of this form (the "null states").

=--

In string-theoretic terms, $P^1$ consists of the on-shell states of the chiral string, and the bracket $[u,v] = u_0 v$ is the action of the [[charge]] $u_0 = \oint Y(u,z)\, d z$ of the weight-1 [[current]] corresponding to $u$.

For $V$ of [[central charge]] 26, the critical value for the [[bosonic string]], the [[no-ghost theorem]] ([Goddard & Thorn 1972](#GoddardThorn72)) identifies the physical states with "transversal" states. Concretely ([Borcherds 1992, Thm. 5.1](#Borcherds92)), suppose that $W$ is a [[vertex operator algebra]] of central charge 24 with positive definite invariant bilinear form, and that
$$
  V \;=\; W \otimes V_{\mathrm{II}_{1,1}}
$$
is its tensor product with the lattice vertex algebra of the even unimodular Lorentzian lattice $\mathrm{II}_{1,1}$ of rank 2. Then $\mathfrak{g}(V)$ is graded by $\mathrm{II}_{1,1}$, with root spaces
$$
  \mathfrak{g}(V)_r
  \;\simeq\;
  W_{1 - r^2/2}
  \,,
  \;\;\;\;
  0 \neq r \in \mathrm{II}_{1,1}
  \,,
$$
the subspaces of $W$ of $L_0$-eigenvalue $1 - r^2/2$. This identification is equivariant for any group of automorphisms of $W$. In particular, the contravariant form on $\mathfrak{g}(V)$ is then positive definite on all nonzero root spaces, so that $\mathfrak{g}(V)$ is a Borcherds algebra by the characterization recalled [above](#Idea). More generally, for every even Lorentzian [[lattice]] $L$, the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}(V_L)$ of physical states of the lattice vertex algebra $V_L$ is a Borcherds algebra ([Borcherds 1988, §5](#Borcherds88), [Borcherds 1995a, Ex. 2](#Borcherds95a)).

The two most prominent examples are the following.

+-- {: .num_example #FakeMonsterLieAlgebra}
###### Example
**(fake Monster Lie algebra)**

The *fake Monster Lie algebra* is
$$
  \mathfrak{g}\big( V_{\mathrm{II}_{25,1}} \big)
  \,,
$$
the Lie algebra of physical states of the lattice vertex algebra of the even unimodular Lorentzian lattice $\mathrm{II}_{25,1}$ of rank 26 ([Borcherds 1990](#Borcherds90), where it is still called "the monster Lie algebra"). Hence it is the Lie algebra of physical states of the chiral bosonic string on the 26-dimensional Lorentzian [[torus]] $\mathbb{R}^{25,1}/\mathrm{II}_{25,1}$.

Its Cartan subalgebra is $\mathrm{II}_{25,1} \otimes \mathbb{R}$, and by the no-ghost theorem the multiplicity of any nonzero $r \in \mathrm{II}_{25,1}$ as a root is $p_{24}(1 - r^2/2)$, where
$$
  \sum_{n} p_{24}(n)\, q^n
  \;=\;
  \prod_{k \geq 1} (1 - q^k)^{-24}
  \,.
$$
So the roots are the nonzero $r$ with $r^2 \leq 2$. Its simple roots are ([Borcherds 1990, Thm. 1](#Borcherds90)):

* the real simple roots: the norm-2 vectors $r \in \mathrm{II}_{25,1}$ with $(r, \rho) = -1$, for $\rho$ a norm-0 Weyl vector; these correspond to the points of the [[Leech lattice]] ([Conway 1983](#Conway83));

* the imaginary simple roots: the positive multiples $n \rho$, $n \geq 1$, each with multiplicity 24.

Accordingly, its denominator identity reads
$$
  e^\rho
  \prod_{r \gt 0}
  \big(1 - e^r\big)^{p_{24}(1 - r^2/2)}
  \;=\;
  \sum_{w \in W}
  \det(w)\,
  w\Big(
    e^\rho
    \prod_{n \geq 1}
    \big(1 - e^{n \rho}\big)^{24}
  \Big)
  \,,
$$
where the product is over the positive roots (the positive multiples of $\rho$ and the roots $r$ with $(r, \rho) \lt 0$), and where $W$ is the [[Weyl group]], here the reflection group of $\mathrm{II}_{25,1}$ generated by the reflections in its norm-2 vectors. On the right, $e^\rho \prod_{n \geq 1}(1 - e^{n\rho})^{24} = \sum_{n} \tau(n)\, e^{n \rho}$ is the [[modular discriminant]] in the variable $e^\rho$, with $\tau$ the Ramanujan tau function.

=--

+-- {: .num_example #MonsterLieAlgebra}
###### Example
**(Monster Lie algebra)**

The *Monster Lie algebra* is ([Borcherds 1992, §6](#Borcherds92))
$$
  \mathfrak{m}
  \;\coloneqq\;
  \mathfrak{g}\big(
    V^\natural \otimes V_{\mathrm{II}_{1,1}}
  \big)
  \,,
$$
the Lie algebra of physical states for the [[moonshine module]] $V^\natural$ ([Frenkel, Lepowsky & Meurman 1988](#FLM88)) tensored with the lattice vertex algebra of $\mathrm{II}_{1,1} \simeq \mathbb{Z}^2$, with inner product $\big((m,n),(m',n')\big) = -(m n' + m' n)$. Hence it is the Lie algebra of physical states of the chiral bosonic string on the product of the "Monster [[CFT]]" with a 2-dimensional Lorentzian torus.

Here $V^\natural = \bigoplus_{k \geq 0} V^\natural_k$ is a vertex operator algebra of central charge 24 with positive definite invariant form, whose automorphism group is the [[monster group]] $\mathbb{M}$, and whose graded dimension is the normalized [[j-invariant]]:
$$
  \sum_{k \geq 0}
  \mathrm{dim}\big( V^\natural_k \big)\, q^{k-1}
  \;=\;
  j(q) - 744
  \;=\;
  \sum_{n \geq -1} c(n)\, q^n
  \;=\;
  q^{-1} + 196884\, q + 21493760\, q^2 + \cdots
  \,.
$$
Therefore, by the no-ghost theorem (as above), $\mathfrak{m}$ is $\mathbb{Z}^2$-graded with root spaces
$$
  \mathfrak{m}_{(m,n)}
  \;\simeq\;
  V^\natural_{1 + m n}
  \;\;\;
  \text{for}
  \;
  (m,n) \neq (0,0)
$$
as $\mathbb{M}$-representations, hence of dimension $c(m n)$, while the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{m}_{(0,0)}$ is 2-dimensional ([Borcherds 1992, Thm. 6.1](#Borcherds92)). The unique real simple root is $(1,-1)$, of norm 2, and the imaginary simple roots are the $(1,n)$ for $n \geq 1$, of norm $-2n$ and with multiplicity $c(n)$ ([Borcherds 1992, Thm. 7.2](#Borcherds92)). Accordingly, writing $p$ and $q$ for the formal exponentials of the degrees $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, the denominator identity of $\mathfrak{m}$ is the product formula
$$
  p^{-1}
  \prod_{m \gt 0, n \in \mathbb{Z}}
  \big(1 - p^m q^n\big)^{c(m n)}
  \;=\;
  j(p) - j(q)
  \,.
$$

Since the Monster group acts on $\mathfrak{m}$ with root spaces the $\mathbb{M}$-representations $V^\natural_{1 + m n}$, there is for each $g \in \mathbb{M}$ a twisted denominator identity ([Borcherds 1992, (8.3)](#Borcherds92)):
$$
  p^{-1}
  \exp\Big(
    -
    \sum_{i \geq 1}
    \sum_{m \geq 1, n \in \mathbb{Z}}
    \mathrm{Tr}\big(g^i \,\vert\, V^\natural_{1 + m n}\big)\,
    \frac{p^{m i}\, q^{n i}}{i}
  \Big)
  \;=\;
  T_g(p) - T_g(q)
  \,,
$$
where
$$
  T_g(q)
  \;\coloneqq\;
  \sum_{n \geq -1}
  \mathrm{Tr}\big(g \,\vert\, V^\natural_{n+1}\big)\, q^n
$$
is the McKay-Thompson series of $g$. For $g = e$ this reduces to the previous identity, since $T_e = j - 744$. These identities imply recursion relations ("replication formulas") which determine each $T_g$ from its first few coefficients. Checking these coefficients proves that the $T_g$ are the Hauptmoduln predicted by Conway and Norton, hence the [[monstrous moonshine]] conjecture ([Borcherds 1992, Thm. 1.1](#Borcherds92)).

=--

In both examples, the denominator identity is the product expansion of an [[automorphic form]]. For the fake Monster Lie algebra this is Borcherds' automorphic form $\Phi_{12}$ of weight 12 for the [[orthogonal group]] of the lattice $\mathrm{II}_{26,2}$ ([Borcherds 1995b](#Borcherds95b)). For the Monster Lie algebra it is $j(p) - j(q)$, a [[modular function]] in each of the variables $p$ and $q$. More generally, the denominator identities of Borcherds algebras are often [[automorphic forms]] on [[orthogonal groups]] $O(s+2,2)$ with infinite product expansions ("Borcherds products", [Borcherds 1995b](#Borcherds95b), [1998](#Borcherds98)). Conversely, hyperbolic Kac-Moody algebras may be enlarged ("automorphically corrected") to Borcherds (super)algebras whose denominator function is an automorphic form ([Gritsenko & Nikulin 1997](#GritsenkoNikulin97)).


### Algebras of BPS States

In [[string theory]], Borcherds algebras appear as algebras of [[BPS states]] ([Harvey & Moore 1996](#HarveyMoore96), [1998](#HarveyMoore98)). For instance, the degeneracies of [[dyons]] in $\mathcal{N}=4$ string compactifications are given in terms of the denominator of a Borcherds superalgebra ([Dijkgraaf, Verlinde & Verlinde 1997](#DVV97)), whose Weyl group governs their [[wall crossing]] ([Cheng & Verlinde 2008](#ChengVerlinde08)). The Monster Lie algebra itself appears as an algebra of BPS states in certain heterotic string models ([Paquette, Persson & Volpato 2016](#PPV16)).


### Organizing SuGra higher gauge fields
 {#OrganizingSugraHigherGaugeFields}

In [[supergravity]], Borcherds superalgebras organize the [[p-form]] [[higher gauge fields]] together with their [[electric-magnetic duality|duals]], via the [[Bianchi identities]] of their [[flux densities]].

**The mechanism.** The [[Bianchi identities]] of the [[flux densities]] in [[supergravity]] are all of the schematic form "$d F = F \wedge F$". This means that they encode a [[Lie superalgebra]] ([Cremmer, Julia, Lü & Pope 1998a](#CJLP98a), [b](#CJLP98b)). To see this, associate to each flux density $F^X_n$ of degree $n$, hence to each potential of degree $p = n - 1$, a generator $e_X$ of degree $p$, which is even or odd according to the parity of $p$. Then all the Bianchi identities combine into the single equation
$$
  d \mathbb{F}
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{2} [\mathbb{F}, \mathbb{F}]
  \,,
  \;\;\;\;\;
  \mathbb{F}
  \;\coloneqq\;
  \sum_X F^X_n\, e_X
  \,,
$$
whose right-hand side defines a graded antisymmetric bracket on the generators. Consistency of the Bianchi identities means that applying $d$ once more makes the right-hand side vanish by itself. This is a cubic condition on the fluxes, and it is equivalent to the graded [[Jacobi identity]] for the bracket ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, §1](#GreitzHowe11b)). In [Cremmer, Julia, Lü & Pope 1998b](#CJLP98b) this leads to a "doubled" formulation of maximal supergravities, in which the [[equations of motion]] become a twisted self-duality condition on the total field strength, valued in a finite-dimensional Lie superalgebra.

These finite-dimensional "duality superalgebras" turn out to be truncations of infinite-dimensional Borcherds superalgebras. For [[torus|toroidal]] [[KK-compactifications]] of [[M-theory]], the root lattices of these Borcherds superalgebras are the second [[integral cohomology]] lattices of [[del Pezzo surfaces]] ([Henry-Labord&#232;re, Julia & Paulot 2002](#HLJP02), [2003](#HLJP03), cf. [[mysterious duality]]). These Borcherds superalgebras may also be derived from [[E11|$E_{11}$]] ([Henneaux, Julia & Levie 2012](#HJL12)).

The truncation is a consequence of working on ordinary spacetime, where forms of degree greater than the spacetime dimension vanish. In [[superspace]], on the other hand, the odd basis 1-forms commute with each other, so that there are non-vanishing forms of arbitrary degree. Accordingly, in the superspace formulation the algebra of all consistent flux densities becomes the full positive part $\mathfrak{n}^+$ of the Borcherds superalgebra ([Greitz & Howe 2011b](#GreitzHowe11b)). Moreover, a superspace cohomology argument shows two further facts ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, §2](#GreitzHowe11b)):

* once the Bianchi identities of the physical fields hold, those of all the higher-degree flux densities are automatically solvable;

* there are no "gauge-trivial" flux densities (those with $d F = 0$) apart from exact 11-forms.

The argument uses the vanishing of the relevant cohomology groups $H^{p,q}_t$ of the dimension-zero torsion for $p \gt 1$ ([Berkovits & Howe 2008](#BerkovitsHowe08)).

The case of [[type IIA supergravity]] illustrates the mechanism most transparently:

+-- {: .num_example #TypeIIAFormAlgebra}
###### Example
**(form algebra of type IIA supergravity)**

The bosonic flux densities of (massless) [[type IIA supergravity]] are

* the [[RR-field|RR-fluxes]] $G_2$, $G_4$ and their duals $G_6$, $G_8$;

* the NS-flux $H_3$ (of the [[B-field]]), its dual $H_7$, and the 9-flux $H_9$ dual to the [[dilaton]];

* the RR 10-flux $G_{10}$, dual to the [[Romans mass]] $G_0$, and NS 11-fluxes $H_{11}$, the field strengths of 10-form potentials.

With [[wedge products]] understood, their [[Bianchi identities]] read ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, (2.13-14)](#GreitzHowe11b)):
$$
  \begin{array}{rcl}
    d H_3 &=& 0
    \\
    d G_{2k+2} &=& H_3 \, G_{2k}
    \mathrlap{\,,
    \;\;\;
    k = 0, \cdots, 4
    \,,
    \;\;\;
    G_0 = 0
    }
    \\
    d H_7 &=& \frac{1}{2} G_4 \, G_4 - G_2 \, G_6
    \\
    d H_9 
      &=& 
    - H_3 \, H_7 
    + \frac{1}{2} G_4 \, G_6 
    - \frac{3}{2} G_2 \, G_8
    \,,
  \end{array}
$$
together with a 2-parameter family of consistent Bianchi identities for $H_{11}$. In the Romans-massive case, $G_0 = m \neq 0$, these are modified to $d G_2 = G_0 \, H_3$, and in the NS identities $G_2 \, G_{2n}$ is replaced by $G_2\, G_{2n} - G_0 \, G_{2n+2}$ for $n \geq 3$ ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, §2.2](#GreitzHowe11b), see also [Lavrinenko, Lü, Pope & Stelle 1999](#LLPS99)).

To find the corresponding Lie superalgebra ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, §4.2](#GreitzHowe11b)), assign the even generator $e_0$ to $H_3$ and the odd generator $e_1$ to $G_2$. Then the $e_0$-component of $d \mathbb{F} = \frac{1}{2}[\mathbb{F}, \mathbb{F}]$ is
$$
  d H_3 \, e_0 \;=\; \frac{1}{2} G_2 \, G_2 \, [e_1, e_1]
  \,,
$$
so that $d H_3 = 0$ requires $[e_1, e_1] = 0$. Writing $e_{01} \coloneqq [e_0, e_1]$, the generators of the higher fluxes are obtained by iterated brackets:

* the RR-fluxes correspond to $G_{2k} \leftrightarrow (\mathrm{ad}_{e_0})^{k-1}(e_1)$, for all $k \geq 1$;

* the further NS-fluxes arise from brackets of these, starting with $H_7 \leftrightarrow [e_{01}, e_{01}]$ and $H_9 \leftrightarrow [e_0, [e_{01}, e_{01}]]$.

The smallest Borcherds superalgebra (in the sense of Remark \ref{VariantsOfTheDefinition}) which accommodates these brackets has generators $e_i, h_i, f_i$ for $i \in \{0,1\}$, with $e_1$ and $f_1$ odd, and Cartan matrix
$$
  \left(
  \array{
    0 & -1
    \\
    -1 & 0
  }
  \right)
  \,.
$$
The entries are fixed as follows:

* $a_{1 1} = 0$ is forced by $[e_1, e_1] = 0$;

* $a_{0 0} = 0$ is forced by the fact that $(\mathrm{ad}_{e_0})^n(e_1) \neq 0$ for all $n$, while there are only finitely many forms of each degree: $a_{0 0} \gt 0$ would contradict the first fact, and $a_{0 0} \lt 0$ would produce a copy of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ with infinite-dimensional representations, contradicting the second;

* $a_{0 1} = a_{1 0} = -1$ is a normalization.

Both simple roots are isotropic, so that $(e_i, h_i, f_i)$ generate Heisenberg (super)algebras.

The algebra of all IIA flux densities is isomorphic to the positive part $\mathfrak{n}^+$ of this Borcherds superalgebra. A flux density of degree $2n + m + 1$ corresponds to a root $n \alpha_0 + m \alpha_1$ as follows ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, §4.2](#GreitzHowe11b)):

| flux density | root | multiplicity | generator |
|--------------|------|--------------|-----------|
| $H_3$ | $\alpha_0$ | $1$ | $e_0$ |
| $G_{2k}$, $\; k \geq 1$ | $(k-1)\alpha_0 + \alpha_1$ | $1$ | $(\mathrm{ad}_{e_0})^{k-1}(e_1)$ |
| $H_{4n+3}$, $\; n \geq 1$ | $2n\, \alpha_0 + 2 \alpha_1$ | $n$ | e.g. $[e_{01}, e_{01}]$ for $n = 1$ |
| $H_{4n+5}$, $\; n \geq 1$ | $(2n+1)\alpha_0 + 2 \alpha_1$ | $n$ | e.g. $[e_0, [e_{01}, e_{01}]]$ for $n = 1$ |

In particular, the multiplicity 2 at $n = 2$ matches the two independent 11-flux and 13-flux Bianchi identities found in the superspace analysis ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, (2.15), (3.8)](#GreitzHowe11b)).

Beyond the spacetime limit, most of these higher fluxes vanish in supergravity, but not all of them. The RR 12-flux $G_{12}$, corresponding to the root $5 \alpha_0 + \alpha_1$, is non-vanishing: its component with ten even and two odd superspace indices is a non-zero multiple of $e^{-\phi}\, \Gamma_{10,2}$, and its Bianchi identity $d G_{12} = H_3 \, G_{10}$ is automatically solved ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, (3.7)](#GreitzHowe11b)). The NS 13-fluxes vanish identically, but their Bianchi identities have non-vanishing terms on the right, whose sum vanishes by the cohomology argument. It has been speculated that such higher-degree forms may become non-zero once string-theoretic higher-order corrections are included ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, §5](#GreitzHowe11b)).

=--

+-- {: .num_remark #FluxDensitiesAsClosedNPlusValuedForms}
###### Remark
**(flux densities as closed $\mathfrak{n}^+$-valued forms)**

Grade $\mathfrak{n}^+$ by the potential degree $p$, so that in the IIA case $p(\alpha_0) = 2$ and $p(\alpha_1) = 1$, whence the super-parity of each root space is $p$ mod 2. This makes $\mathfrak{n}^+$ a [[graded Lie algebra|$\mathbb{Z}$-graded Lie algebra]] concentrated in positive degrees, hence an [[L-infinity algebra]] with only a binary bracket. Up to the sign convention $\mathbb{F} \mapsto - \mathbb{F}$, the combined Bianchi identity $d \mathbb{F} = \frac{1}{2}[\mathbb{F}, \mathbb{F}]$ then says that the flux densities constitute a closed [[infinity-Lie algebra valued differential form|$\mathfrak{n}^+$-valued differential form]], hence a [[dg-algebra]] homomorphism
$$
  \mathrm{CE}\big(\mathfrak{n}^+\big)
  \longrightarrow
  \Omega^\bullet_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)
$$
out of the [[Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra]] of $\mathfrak{n}^+$. In the CE algebra, the generators dual to root spaces of potential degree $p$ sit in degree $p+1$. This is the form in which Bianchi identities enter [[flux quantization]].

For example, in the IIA case the root spaces whose roots have $\alpha_1$-coefficient $\geq 2$ span an [[ideal]] in $\mathfrak{n}^+$. The quotient by this ideal retains only $H_3$ and the RR-fluxes $G_{2k}$, with their Bianchi identities $d H_3 = 0$ and $d G_{2k+2} = H_3 \, G_{2k}$, which are those of the twisted [[Chern character]] in [[twisted K-theory]].

+-- {: .num_example #TypeIIBFormAlgebra}
###### Example
**(form algebra of type IIB supergravity)**

All flux densities of [[type IIB supergravity]] have odd degree, so that its Borcherds algebra is purely even. Its Cartan matrix is
$$
  \left(
  \array{
    0 & -1
    \\
    -1 & 2
  }
  \right)
  \,,
$$
which gives the rank-2 Borcherds algebra previously considered by [Slansky 1993](#Slansky93) ([Henry-Labord&#232;re, Julia & Paulot 2002](#HLJP02), [Greitz & Howe 2011b, §4.1](#GreitzHowe11b)). Its two simple roots play the following roles:

* the real simple root $\alpha_1$ generates a copy of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$, the (complexified) Lie algebra of the [[U-duality]] group $SL(2,\mathbb{R})$, with $e_1$ associated with the [[axion]];

* the isotropic simple root $\alpha_0$ corresponds to the lowest weight of the doublet of 3-fluxes.

Flux densities of degree $2n+1$ sit at level $n$, the coefficient of $\alpha_0$. Up to level 7 their $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$-content is
$$
  \mathbf{2}
  ,\;\;
  \mathbf{1}
  ,\;\;
  \mathbf{2}
  ,\;\;
  \mathbf{3}
  ,\;\;
  \mathbf{4} + \mathbf{2}
  ,\;\;
  \mathbf{5} + \mathbf{3} + \mathbf{1}
  ,\;\;
  \mathbf{6} + 2 \cdot \mathbf{4} + 2 \cdot \mathbf{2}
  \,.
$$
This matches, including the multiplicities of the 15-fluxes, the consistent Bianchi identities found in superspace ([Greitz & Howe 2011b, §3.1, §4.1](#GreitzHowe11b)).

=--

Analogous statements hold in other dimensions:

* For maximal supergravity in $D$ dimensions, $3 \leq D \leq 11$, the dynamical $p$-forms correspond to (a truncation of) a real Borcherds superalgebra whose Cartan matrix is given explicitly for each $D$, and which may be derived from [[E11|$E_{11}$]] ([Henneaux, Julia & Levie 2012](#HJL12), see also [Kleinschmidt & Palmkvist 2013](#KleinschmidtPalmkvist13)).

* The superspace analysis of form algebras has been carried out for (half-)maximal supergravity in $3 \leq D \leq 11$ ([Howe & Palmkvist 2015](#HowePalmkvist15); for $D = 3$ see [Greitz & Howe 2011a](#GreitzHowe11a), [2012](#GreitzHowe12)). There, the resulting Borcherds superalgebras are shown to yield the same form spectrum as associated Kac-Moody algebras, which is $E_{11}$ in the maximal case (see also [Palmkvist 2012b](#Palmkvist12b)).

* Gauging deforms the Bianchi identities by the [[embedding tensor]], which extends the form algebra to a [[tensor hierarchy algebra]] ([Palmkvist 2014](#Palmkvist14), [Greitz, Howe & Palmkvist 2014](#GHP14), [Howe & Palmkvist 2015](#HowePalmkvist15)). In this way Borcherds superalgebras encode the [[tensor hierarchies]] of [[gauged supergravity]] ([Palmkvist 2012a](#Palmkvist12)).

* Borcherds superalgebras also govern the gauge structure of [[exceptional generalized geometry]] ([Palmkvist 2015](#Palmkvist15)), in the form of [[L-infinity algebras|$L_\infty$-algebras]] ([Cederwall & Palmkvist 2019](#CederwallPalmkvist19), [Lavau & Palmkvist 2020](#LavauPalmkvist20)).
