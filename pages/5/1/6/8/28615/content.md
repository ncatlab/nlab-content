## Idea

*Lazard elimination* ([Lazard 1960](#Lazard60), [Bourbaki 1972, §II.2.9](#Bourbaki)) is the statement that the [[free Lie algebra]] $\mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W)$ on a [[direct sum]] of [[modules]] is the [[semidirect product]] of the free Lie algebra $\mathfrak{L}(V)$ with the free Lie algebra on the module $T(V) \otimes W$, where $T(V)$ denotes the [[tensor algebra]] on $V$ (Prop. \ref{LazardElimination} below):

$$
  \mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W)
  \;\simeq\;
  \mathfrak{L}(V)
  \ltimes
  \mathfrak{L}\big(T(V) \otimes W\big)
  \,.
$$

In particular, the [[Lie ideal]] generated by $W$ is itself a free Lie algebra, freely generated by the iterated brackets $\big[v_1, [v_2, \cdots, [v_n, w] \cdots]\big]$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $v_i \in V$, $w \in W$.

In words: the generators in $V$ may be "eliminated", at the cost of trading the generators in $W$ for all their iterated brackets with elements of $V$ (typically infinitely many even if $V$ and $W$ are of finite rank).

Iterating this elimination is a basic tool for constructing [[basis|bases]] of free Lie algebras and for exhibiting free Lie subalgebras ([Viennot 1978](#Viennot78), [Reutenauer 1993](#Reutenauer93)).

Conceptually, Lazard elimination is the special case $\mathfrak{h} = \mathfrak{L}(V)$ of the fact that the free product of any Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}$ with a free Lie algebra $\mathfrak{L}(W)$ is the semidirect product $\mathfrak{h} \ltimes \mathfrak{L}\big(U(\mathfrak{h}) \otimes W\big)$ (Prop. \ref{FreeProductWithFreeLieAlgebra} below, cf. [Block & Leroux 1972](#BlockLeroux72), [Lepowsky & Wilson 1995](#LepowskyWilson95)). In this form it has found notable application to [[Borcherds algebras]] (Rem. \ref{ApplicationToBorcherdsAlgebras} below).


## Statement

Throughout, $R$ denotes a [[commutative ring]], "[[module]]" means $R$-module, and [[Lie algebras]] are over $R$ (with $[x,x] = 0$ for all $x$). We write

* $\mathfrak{L} \colon Mod_R \longrightarrow LieAlg_R$ for the [[free Lie algebra]]-functor, [[left adjoint]] to the [[forgetful functor]];

* $T(-)$ for the [[tensor algebra]]-functor and $U(-)$ for the [[universal enveloping algebra]]-functor, so that $U\big(\mathfrak{L}(V)\big) \simeq T(V)$ (both being left adjoint to the forgetful functor from [[associative algebras]] to modules);

* $\mathfrak{L}(Y) \coloneqq \mathfrak{L}\big(R^{\oplus Y}\big)$ for the free Lie algebra on a [[set]] $Y$ of generators.

Given a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}$ and an $\mathfrak{h}$-[[Lie algebra representation|module]] $M$, the action of each $h \in \mathfrak{h}$ on $M$ extends uniquely to a [[derivation]] $D_h$ of $\mathfrak{L}(M)$ (derivations of a free Lie algebra being freely determined by their values on generators), and $h \mapsto D_h$ is a Lie homomorphism $\mathfrak{h} \to Der\big(\mathfrak{L}(M)\big)$ (since $[D_h, D_{h'}]$ and $D_{[h,h']}$ are derivations which agree on generators). This defines the [[semidirect product]] Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h} \ltimes \mathfrak{L}(M)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{FreeProductWithFreeLieAlgebra}
**(free product with a free Lie algebra)**
For $\mathfrak{h}$ a Lie algebra and $W$ a module, the [[coproduct]] ("free product") of $\mathfrak{h}$ with $\mathfrak{L}(W)$ is the semidirect product of $\mathfrak{h}$ with the free Lie algebra on the free $U(\mathfrak{h})$-module on $W$:

$$
  \mathfrak{h} \sqcup \mathfrak{L}(W)
  \;\simeq\;
  \mathfrak{h}
  \ltimes
  \mathfrak{L}\big(U(\mathfrak{h}) \otimes W\big)
  \,.
$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, expanding out the bracket of the semidirect product shows that Lie homomorphisms $\mathfrak{h} \ltimes \mathfrak{L}(M) \to \mathfrak{g}$ are equivalently pairs of Lie homomorphisms $\phi \colon \mathfrak{h} \to \mathfrak{g}$ and $\psi \colon \mathfrak{L}(M) \to \mathfrak{g}$ satisfying

$$
  \psi\big(D_h(n)\big)
  \;=\;
  \big[\phi(h),\, \psi(n)\big]
  \;\;\;\;\;\;
  \text{for all}\; h \in \mathfrak{h},\, n \in \mathfrak{L}(M)
  \,.
$$

For fixed $h$, both sides of this equation are $\psi$-derivations in $n$, so that the locus of $n$ on which it holds is a [[Lie subalgebra]]. Therefore the condition holds as soon as it holds for all $n \in M$, which means that the restriction $\psi\vert_M$ is a homomorphism of $\mathfrak{h}$-modules from $M$ to $\mathfrak{g}$, the latter regarded as an $\mathfrak{h}$-module via $\mathrm{ad} \circ \phi$. For $M = U(\mathfrak{h}) \otimes W$ the free $U(\mathfrak{h})$-module on $W$, such module homomorphisms are in turn equivalent to plain linear maps $W \to \mathfrak{g}$, hence to Lie homomorphisms $\mathfrak{L}(W) \to \mathfrak{g}$. In summary, there are bijections

$$
  \array{
    Hom_{LieAlg}\Big(
      \mathfrak{h} \ltimes \mathfrak{L}\big(U(\mathfrak{h}) \otimes W\big)
      ,\,
      \mathfrak{g}
    \Big)
    &\simeq&
    Hom_{LieAlg}\big(\mathfrak{h},\, \mathfrak{g}\big)
    \times
    Hom_{LieAlg}\big(\mathfrak{L}(W),\, \mathfrak{g}\big)
    \\
    &\simeq&
    Hom_{LieAlg}\big(
      \mathfrak{h} \sqcup \mathfrak{L}(W)
      ,\,
      \mathfrak{g}
    \big)
  }
$$

[[natural transformation|natural]] in $\mathfrak{g}$, and the claim follows by the [[Yoneda lemma]].
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{LazardElimination}
**(Lazard elimination)**
For $V, W$ modules, there is an isomorphism of Lie algebras

$$
  \mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W)
  \;\simeq\;
  \mathfrak{L}(V)
  \ltimes
  \mathfrak{L}\big(T(V) \otimes W\big)
  \,,
$$

where $\mathfrak{L}(V)$ acts on $T(V) \otimes W$ by left multiplication on the first tensor factor (via $\mathfrak{L}(V) \hookrightarrow U\big(\mathfrak{L}(V)\big) \simeq T(V)$), given on generators by

$$
  \array{
    v &\mapsto& (v,\, 0)
    \\
    w &\mapsto& (0,\, 1 \otimes w)
  }
$$

with inverse given on generators by

$$
  \array{
    (v,\, 0) &\mapsto& v
    \\
    \big(0,\, v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n \otimes w\big)
    &\mapsto&
    \big[v_1, [v_2, \cdots, [v_n, w] \cdots]\big]
    \,.
  }
$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\mathfrak{L}$ is a left adjoint, it preserves [[coproducts]], $\mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W) \simeq \mathfrak{L}(V) \sqcup \mathfrak{L}(W)$, so that the isomorphism is the special case of Prop. \ref{FreeProductWithFreeLieAlgebra} for $\mathfrak{h} \coloneqq \mathfrak{L}(V)$. The formulas follow by tracing identity morphisms through the bijections in the proof there. For instance, the inverse map corresponds to the canonical inclusion $\mathfrak{L}(V) \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W)$ together with the $T(V)$-linear extension of $W \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W)$, which sends $v_1 \cdots v_n \otimes w$ to $\mathrm{ad}_{v_1} \circ \cdots \circ \mathrm{ad}_{v_n}(w)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{ClassicalFormulation}
**(classical formulation)**
Under the isomorphism of Prop. \ref{LazardElimination}, the projection onto $\mathfrak{L}(V)$ corresponds to $\mathfrak{L}(pr_V)$, whose [[kernel]] is the [[Lie ideal]] $\langle W \rangle$ generated by $W$. Hence Lazard elimination says equivalently that:

1. $\langle W \rangle \subset \mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W)$ is the free Lie algebra on $T(V) \otimes W \simeq \underset{n \in \mathbb{N}}{\bigoplus} V^{\otimes n} \otimes W$, embedded via $v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n \otimes w \mapsto \big[v_1, [v_2, \cdots, [v_n,w]\cdots]\big]$;

1. as $R$-modules, $\mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W) \,\simeq\, \mathfrak{L}(V) \oplus \langle W \rangle$.

For free modules on sets $Y$ and $Z$, this is the form in which the statement is traditionally given ([Bourbaki 1972, §II.2.9, Prop. 10](#Bourbaki)):

$$
  \mathfrak{L}(Y \sqcup Z)
  \;=\;
  \mathfrak{L}(Y)
  \,\oplus\,
  \mathfrak{L}\Big(
    \big\{
      [y_1, [y_2, \cdots, [y_n, z] \cdots]]
      \,\big\vert\,
      n \in \mathbb{N},\, y_i \in Y,\, z \in Z
    \big\}
  \Big)
  \,,
$$

where the first summand is a Lie subalgebra and the second is the Lie ideal generated by $Z$.
\end{remark}


## Examples

\begin{example}\label{EliminatingASingleGenerator}
**(eliminating a single generator)**
For $V = R x$ free of rank 1, we have $\mathfrak{L}(R x) = R x$ (since $[x,x] = 0$) and $T(R x) \simeq R[x]$, so that Prop. \ref{LazardElimination} gives

$$
  \mathfrak{L}(R x \oplus W)
  \;\simeq\;
  R x
  \,\ltimes\,
  \mathfrak{L}\big(R[x] \otimes W\big)
  \,,
$$

where $x^n \otimes w$ corresponds to $\mathrm{ad}^n_x(w) = [x, [x, \cdots, [x, w]\cdots]]$. For instance, in the free Lie algebra $\mathfrak{L}\big(\{x,y\}\big)$ on two generators, the Lie ideal generated by $y$ is the free Lie algebra on the countably many generators $\big\{\mathrm{ad}^n_x(y)\big\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}\label{DerivedSubalgebraOfFreeLieAlgebraOnTwoGenerators}
**(derived subalgebra of the free Lie algebra on two generators)**
Applying Ex. \ref{EliminatingASingleGenerator} once more, now eliminating the generator $y = \mathrm{ad}^0_x(y)$ from $\big\{\mathrm{ad}^n_x(y)\big\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, gives a decomposition of $R$-modules

$$
  \mathfrak{L}\big(\{x,y\}\big)
  \;\simeq\;
  R x \,\oplus\, R y \,\oplus\, \mathfrak{L}(S)
  \,,
  \;\;\;\;\;
  S
  \;\coloneqq\;
  \Big\{
    \mathrm{ad}^m_y \, \mathrm{ad}^n_x(y)
  \Big\}_{ m \in \mathbb{N},\, n \geq 1 }
  \,.
$$

Since the [[derived Lie algebra|derived subalgebra]] $[\mathfrak{L}, \mathfrak{L}]$ contains $S$ and has trivial intersection with the degree-1 summand $R x \oplus R y$, it follows that $[\mathfrak{L}, \mathfrak{L}] = \mathfrak{L}(S)$ is free on $S$.
\end{example}


## Properties

\begin{remark}\label{BasesOfFreeLieAlgebras}
**(bases of free Lie algebras)**
For $X$ a [[finite set]], repeatedly eliminating (Ex. \ref{EliminatingASingleGenerator}) a free generator of lowest degree, all remaining free generators eventually have degree exceeding any given bound. Hence the eliminated generators form a [[basis]] of $\mathfrak{L}(X)$ as an $R$-module; in particular $\mathfrak{L}(X)$ is a [[free module|free]] $R$-module. This is the *Lazard elimination process*. The resulting "Lazard sets" coincide with the *Hall sets* which index *Hall bases*, including the *Lyndon basis* ([Viennot 1978](#Viennot78), cf. [Reutenauer 1993](#Reutenauer93)).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{Variants}
**(variants)**

* For [[universal enveloping algebras]] over a [[field]], using $U(\mathfrak{h} \ltimes \mathfrak{n}) \simeq U(\mathfrak{n}) \otimes U(\mathfrak{h})$, Lazard elimination gives $T(V \oplus W) \simeq T\big(T(V) \otimes W\big) \otimes T(V)$. On bases of words this is the unique factorization $(Y \sqcup Z)^\ast \simeq (Y^\ast Z)^\ast \times Y^\ast$ of words in letters from $Y \sqcup Z$. The [[Hopf algebra|Hopf-algebraic]] formulation of elimination goes back to [Block & Leroux 1972](#BlockLeroux72), see [Lepowsky & Wilson 1995](#LepowskyWilson95).

* The analogous statement for [[free groups]] (with the same proof, using $G$-[[sets]] in place of modules) says that the free product of a [[group]] $G$ with the free group $F(Z)$ on a set $Z$ is the semidirect product $G \ltimes F(G \times Z)$. For instance, the [[normal subgroup]] of $F(Y \sqcup Z)$ generated by $Z$ is free on the conjugates $g z g^{-1}$, for $g \in F(Y)$ and $z \in Z$.

* There are versions for [[restricted Lie algebras]] ([Stöhr 2001](#Stohr01)), for free partially commutative Lie algebras ([Duchamp & Krob 1993](#DuchampKrob93)), and for quotients of $\mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W)$ by suitable ideals ([Jurisich & Wilson 2004](#JurisichWilson04)).

* The proofs above apply verbatim to [[graded Lie algebras]] over $\mathbb{Q}$ (with [[Koszul sign rule|Koszul signs]]).

\end{remark}


\begin{remark}\label{RelationToHiltonMilnorTheorem}
**(relation to the Hilton-Milnor theorem)**
For [[connected topological space|connected]] pointed [[CW-complexes]] $X$, $Y$, the first step of the [[Hilton-Milnor theorem]] is the [[weak homotopy equivalence]]

$$
  \Omega \Sigma (X \vee Y)
  \;\simeq\;
  \Omega \Sigma X
  \,\times\,
  \Omega \Sigma \Big(
    \underset{n \in \mathbb{N}}{\bigvee}
    X^{\wedge n} \wedge Y
  \Big)
  \,.
$$

The [[rational homotopy theory|rational homotopy groups]] $\pi_\bullet(\Omega \Sigma Z) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ form, under the [[Samelson product]], the free graded Lie algebra on $\widetilde{H}_\bullet(Z;\mathbb{Q})$. Moreover, $\widetilde{H}_\bullet\big(\bigvee_n X^{\wedge n} \wedge Y; \mathbb{Q}\big) \simeq T(V) \otimes W$ for $V \coloneqq \widetilde H_\bullet(X;\mathbb{Q})$ and $W \coloneqq \widetilde H_\bullet(Y;\mathbb{Q})$. Hence this equivalence is a space-level counterpart of the underlying module decomposition $\mathfrak{L}(V \oplus W) \simeq \mathfrak{L}(V) \oplus \mathfrak{L}\big(T(V) \otimes W\big)$ of graded Lazard elimination. Iterating it yields the full Hilton-Milnor theorem, with factors indexed by a Hall basis of the free Lie algebra on two generators (cf. Rem. \ref{BasesOfFreeLieAlgebras}).
\end{remark}


## Applications

\begin{remark}\label{ApplicationToBorcherdsAlgebras}
**(to Borcherds algebras)**
Using Lazard elimination, [Jurisich 1998](#Jurisich98) showed that every [[Borcherds algebra]] $\mathfrak{g}$ without mutually orthogonal imaginary simple roots decomposes as

$$
  \mathfrak{g}
  \;=\;
  \mathfrak{u}^+
  \oplus
  (\mathfrak{g}_J + \mathfrak{h})
  \oplus
  \mathfrak{u}^-
  \,,
$$

where $\mathfrak{g}_J$ is a [[Kac-Moody algebra]] and $\mathfrak{u}^\pm$ are free Lie algebras on explicit generating modules. For the Monster Lie algebra $\mathfrak{m}$ of [[monstrous moonshine]] this gives $\mathfrak{m} = \mathfrak{u}^+ \oplus \mathfrak{gl}_2 \oplus \mathfrak{u}^-$. Seen through semidirect products $\mathfrak{h} \ltimes \mathfrak{L}(M)$ as in Prop. \ref{FreeProductWithFreeLieAlgebra}, with $\mathfrak{gl}_2$ in the role of $\mathfrak{h}$, this becomes transparent ([Lepowsky & Wilson 1995](#LepowskyWilson95)). The free Lie algebra structure of $\mathfrak{u}^-$ serves to simplify part of [[Richard Borcherds|Borcherds]]' proof of the Conway-Norton conjectures ([Jurisich, Lepowsky & Wilson 1994](#JurisichLepowskyWilson94)).
\end{remark}
