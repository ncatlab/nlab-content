## Idea

It is common that [[quantum field theories]] are conceived incompletely (often as nothing but [[Lagrangian densities]]) and as such require further *completion* of their definition.

There are broadly two sectors that require special attention in this regard, concerning the definition and dynamics of [[quantum fields]]: at very 

1. small [[length]] [[scales]]: "*in the UV*",

1. large [[length scales]]: "*in the IR*",

where this jargon is a historical echo of the fact that:

* *ultraviolet* (UV) [[light]] has small [[wavelengths]], 

* *infrared* (IR) [[light]] has large [[wavelengths]]

compared to visible light. 

But the [[length]] [[scales]] involved in UV/IR completions of quantum field theories are usually many orders of magnitude away from the wavelengths of visible light, in either direction, often to the extreme that: 

* UV-completion aims at the [[Planck length|Planck scale]],

* IR-completion aims at [[cosmology|cosmic]] scales.

At the same time, neither term is reflected much in the literature at all, nor even well defined, even by [[physics]] standards. The term "UV completion" is prominent in informal discussion of [[quantum gravity]], where theories like [[string theory]] (not themselves QFTs!) are advertised as providing UV-completions of [[gravity|gravitational]] and [[gauge field theories]] . The term "IR-completion" has not been used much at all; but see the [references below](#References)
 
One fairly precise use of the term "UV-complete" is in the sense of [[asymptotic safety]], hence in the sense that the [[renormalization group flow]] of a [[perturbative quantum field theory]] never diverges in the direction of smaller length scales. The authors [CLPPR 2014](#CLPPR2014) use the term "IR-completion" (somewhat implicitly, after their equation (9)) in the same sense but sign-reversed sense, for [[renormalization group flow|RG-flows]] to large length scales.

However, this is far from being the universally accepted meaning of the term(s). Indeed, [[string theory|string theorists]] --- probably the largest group of users of the term "UV-completion" in informal writing --- typically claim both that [[string theory]] provides UV-completions and that this is *incompatible* with [[asymptotic safety]] (see [there](asymptotic+safety#TheoreticalIssues)).

An evident part of the terminological problem is a glaring conceptual gap in [[quantum field theory]]: Precisely defined and nontrivial examples of complete QFTs in any sense of the word --- the topic of *[[constructive field theory]]* --- are notoriously elusive (cf. the *[[Yang-Mills mass gap]] [millennium problem](Yang-Mills+mass+gap#ReferencesMassGapProblem)*) and this situation only becomes worse when going beyond the scope of QFTs into [[string theory]] (cf. the *[[M-theory]] conjecture*).

### On UV-completion

A rare example of an article going beyond just dropping the term, by [[philosophy of science|philosophers of science]]:

* Karen Crowther, Niels Linnemann: *Renormalizability, fundamentality and a final theory: The role of UV-completion in the search for quantum gravity*, Brit. J. Phil. Sci. **70** 2 (2019) 377--406 \[<a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axx052">doi:10.1093/bjps/axx052</a>, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06777">arXiv:1705.06777</a>\]

The usual [[folklore]] verbiage used in [[string theory]] appears in the abstract of:

* Houri-Christina Tarazi: *UV Completeness: From Quantum Field Theory to Quantum Gravity*, PhD thesis, Harvard (2023) \[<a href="https://nbn-resolving.org//URN-3:HUL.INSTREPOS:37378030">URN-3:HUL.INSTREPOS:37378030</a>\]
  > "Abstract: One of the main goals of theoretical physics is to describe physics at all energy scales. The usual renormalization scheme of quantum field theory provides a way to investigate the details of physical systems at different energy scales, potentially up to some energy cut-off. When a theory is predictive at arbitrarily high energies and hence the cut-off can be removed, we say the theory is UV complete. The importance of UV completeness lies in its ability to explain physical phenomena at arbitrarily small distances or high energies. However, when standard techniques from quantum field theory fail to provide such a description, new degrees of freedom are expected to become important. In this thesis, we investigate two types of UV completions; one associated with conformal field theories and the other with quantum gravity. For the former, we study the fixed points of the renormalization group where one needs to integrate-in light solitonic particles/strings in the context of supersymmetric conformal field theories. For the latter, we review the challenges of UV completing quantum gravity and the severe constraints that such a completion is expected to provide for the low-energy physics. \[...\]"
